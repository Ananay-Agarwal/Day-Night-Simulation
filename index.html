Introduction

The goal was to implement a real‑time day‑night lighting simulation inside a custom ray tracer, without relying on a pre‑built game engine. Key objectives:

  • Dynamically change sky color, light color, and light intensity based on the sun’s elevation.
  • Smooth transition: sunrise → day → sunset → night with realistic hues (blue sky, orange/red twilight, dark night).
  • Add sun and moon spheres as visual references.
  • Ensure shadows and reflections respond correctly to the changing light.
  • Provide an interactive pause feature to freeze the cycle for inspection.

The final deliverable is a live, GPU‑accelerated ray tracer written with Taichi, where the user can orbit the camera and watch a full day‑night cycle in real time (>90 FPS on an RTX 4060).

Architecture

The project built upon an existing real‑time path tracer (diffuse, mirror, dielectric, emissive materials) and added a time‑of‑day system without breaking performance or material logic.

Core decisions

  • No spectral ray tracing – Instead of expensive wavelength‑dependent rendering, a physically plausible color mixing approach based on sun elevation keeps the ray tracer real‑time (≥90 fps on RTX 4060).
  • Dynamic light source – The main directional light (sun/moon) moves on an elliptical orbit around the scene center. Its position determines the elevation angle, which controls light color (warm/cool), intensity, and sky gradient.
  • Separate visual sun/moon spheres – Two spheres with an EMISSIVE material are placed far away (20 units) in the light direction and opposite. Radii set to zero when not visible.
  • Pause mechanism – Spacebar toggle freezes the time variable t (sun angle, light color, sky) while camera movement continues, allowing detailed inspection.
Performance considerations
• All heavy computations (ray marching, shading, shadow rays) run on GPU via Taichi kernels.
• Day‑night logic runs on CPU per frame (negligible overhead).
• Only two extra spheres (sun/moon) → minimal increase in ray tracing cost.

System

1. Complete ray tracer core

The ray tracer implements a recursive path tracer with the following components:

Materials
• Diffuse (Lambertian)
• Mirror (perfect specular)
• Dielectric (Fresnel + refraction)
• Emissive (sun/moon glow)
Intersection
• Sphere / infinite ground plane
• World traversal (closest hit)
Shading & lighting
• Shadow rays with attenuation for transparent/mirror occluders (Beer‑Lambert)
• Ambient term + specular highlight
Recursive tracing
• Max depth 15
• Diffuse + direct lighting + random bounce
• Mirror/dielectric Fresnel & throughput

2. Time‑of‑day system (scene_2.py)

  • Sun/Moon orbit – t increments by 0.005 per frame → full cycle ≈21 seconds. Sun position follows an ellipse: light_x = cos(angle)*radius, light_y = sin(angle)*y_amp.
  • Elevation & day factor – elevation = sun_dir.y, day_factor = smoothstep(-0.15, 0.25, elevation) (0 = night, 1 = full day).
  • Light color mixing – Sun: (1.0,0.95,0.85)×1.2; Moon: (0.65,0.75,1.0)×0.8; Sunset: (1.0,0.5,0.2)×5.0. Blended with elevation‑based sunset tint.
  • Ambient – Day (0.15,0.15,0.20), night (0.02,0.03,0.05), mixed by day_factor.
  • Dynamic sky gradient – In the miss shader: top/bottom colors interpolate between night & day variants; orange tint added near sunrise/sunset.
  • Sun/Moon spheres (visual only) – Distance 20 units; radius 0.8 (sun when visible) / 0.7 (moon). Emissive color added directly.
  • Pause – window.get_key(ti.ui.SPACE) toggles time freezing, camera remains active.

3. Performance

>90 FPS on RTX 4060 at 800×600 resolution, 15 bounces. Taichi’s CUDA backend compiles kernels to efficient GPU code. No visible lag during day‑night transitions.

Known issues / Limitations

  • Shadows at night are very faint – Low ambient + high shadow attenuation makes them nearly invisible on dark surfaces. (Future fix: exposure compensation or stronger moonlight.)
  • Sun & moon spheres disappear abruptly – Instant radius change causes popping instead of a smooth fade below the horizon. They also vanish in mid‑air because they are rendered at a fixed 20‑unit distance.
  • No perspective horizon sink – Spheres do not shrink as they approach the horizon; they appear as large glowing balls moving at constant angular speed. Placing them at 1000+ units would fix this.

Future Work

  • Spectral ray tracing – Simulate true atmospheric scattering (Rayleigh/Mie) for physically accurate sky and caustics.
  • Realistic sun/moon horizon behavior – Smooth fade over elevation range (-0.1 to 0.1) and distance scaling.
  • Dynamic exposure – Auto‑adjust brightness to make night shadows visible while keeping moonlight realistic.
  • Moon phases – Independent orbit and illumination fraction.
  • Stars, UI slider, tone mapping, clouds, volumetric atmosphere – Complete cinematic experience.
  • Comparative analysis – Benchmark against Unity/Unreal Engine for performance & realism trade‑offs.
Conclusion – This project successfully demonstrates a believable, real‑time day‑night cycle in a fully custom ray tracer, with interactive camera and pause. The architecture is lightweight enough to run >90 FPS on an RTX 4060 while matching expected lighting behaviour from morning to night.
